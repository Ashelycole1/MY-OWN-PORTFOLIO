Legal information should not require a stable internet connection to access. In Uganda, where large swaths of the population live in areas with unreliable connectivity, that assumption is a barrier — and Law Buddy was built to remove it.

## The constraint that defined the architecture

The 1995 Ugandan Constitution is the core of the app. It is a fixed document: it does not change day-to-day, it does not require a live API, and it can be bundled entirely. That single fact made offline-first not just feasible but the obvious right choice.

The design principle: **if you download the app once, it should work forever without touching the network.**

## How offline storage works

Law Buddy uses the browser's built-in storage APIs — specifically a combination of service workers and IndexedDB — to cache the entire constitution on first load. After that initial fetch:

- **Service workers** intercept all network requests and serve from cache.
- **IndexedDB** stores the searchable article data, enabling full-text search without any server round-trips.
- The app shell (UI chrome, fonts, styles) is pre-cached at install time.

The result: after a single online visit, the app is fully functional on a plane, in a rural area, or anywhere with zero signal.

## Structuring 275 articles for readability

The 1995 Ugandan Constitution has 287 articles across 19 chapters, plus schedules. Dumping this as raw text would be unusable. Law Buddy structures it as:

- Chapter navigation (sidebar/drawer)
- Article-level deep links (shareable URLs like `/article/29` for Freedom of Expression)
- Search that matches article titles and full text

Each article is stored as a discrete record in IndexedDB, which makes both navigation and search fast regardless of connection state.

## What I learned

Building without a network dependency forced a different kind of discipline. Every feature had to answer the question: *does this work offline?* That filter eliminated a lot of unnecessary complexity and kept the bundle lean.

The other lesson: accessibility and offline availability are deeply related. Offline-first is, in many contexts, just another word for accessible.
